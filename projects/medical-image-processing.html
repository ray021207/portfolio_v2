Why the pipeline matters

A DICOM radiograph, a JPEG and a labeled segmentation mask carry different information. Understanding acquisition formats, spatial coordinates and mask alignment is a necessary foundation for trustworthy medical computer vision.

What the notebook covers

The repository contains nine exercises: PNG/JPEG and DICOM input; direction classification with ResNet50; gender classification with ResNet18; X-ray age regression; lung segmentation using U-Net; region extraction from segmentation masks; bounding-box localization; autoencoder reconstruction error; and ResNet-feature clustering with K-means.

How the exercises run

The code is designed for Google Colab with supplementary archives from medical-imaging-style datasets. The notebook demonstrates reusable processing steps rather than presenting a single clinically validated model.

What I'd verify next

Patient-disjoint test sets, independent radiograph collections and careful subgroup analysis would be needed before interpreting any exercises as evidence of clinical performance.

Interpretation & limits

Instructional coursework. No clinical deployment, independent medical validation or certified diagnostic results are claimed.
