The problem

Underground and indoor environments remove GPS as a reliable source of position. A robot must instead estimate its motion from onboard sensors and map what it encounters. The challenge is that sensor readings are not guaranteed to be usable even when the simulator is running normally.

I started by building a controlled environment in which perception failures could be measured, rather than treating a plausible-looking robot path as evidence of a reliable navigation system.

The experimental setup

The project uses an approximately 48 × 35 metre simulated cave, a Spot-like robot in Isaac Sim, RTX LiDAR and a high-rate IMU. ROS 2 Jazzy coordinates the navigation and sensor-processing work.

One early fault surfaced through LiDAR minimum-range configuration: 326 rays were flagged as invalid in the diagnostic scenario. A separate short-term orientation check found simulated IMU heading drift no greater than 0.21° over 15 seconds. These are isolated sensor checks, not proof of accurate full-map localization.

What matters next

The next steps are repeatable failure injection, explicit map-confidence estimates, and tests where drift, missing returns and scene ambiguity happen together. A genuinely useful system needs to say when its map is degrading.

This is ongoing simulation research. There is no claim of validated field autonomy or a published full navigation benchmark.

What this evidence supports

In progress. The repository currently does not provide a public reproducible implementation; the figures above describe controlled simulator diagnostics, not an end-to-end performance result.
