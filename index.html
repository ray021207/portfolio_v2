02 / The professional journey

A path made
by trying things.

I started with hands-on assistive technology, found myself building tools for people's health, and grew into research that asks harder questions about human behavior and machine perception.

01
2025The starting point

Tikkun Olam Makers

Assistive-technology team project · ASU

Leading a five-person team on a wearable fall-alert ring taught me to start with the person using the technology, not just the technology itself.

What I learned ↗

Prototyping wearable hardware involves trade-offs in comfort, sensing, feasibility and reliability. This was a student community project, not a separate employer.

02
2026 — PresentFreelance engineering

Monark Health

Health Tracker · One client project, one role

I worked on a patient-facing health-tracking and care-planning product, connecting a React interface to a backend and AI-assisted workflows. The challenge was making the system useful, consistent and careful with sensitive data.

What I built ↗

React clinic portal and patient web app, Express / PostgreSQL services, validated care-plan generation, and security controls. Production launch remains pending BAA approval. “Health Tracker” and “Monark Health” refer to this same freelance work.

03 / About

I'd rather
be curious.

M.S. Robotics & Autonomous Systems (AI), Arizona State University
B.S. Artificial Intelligence & Machine Learning, REVA University

I've always been drawn to nature, but my education took me into AI, computer vision, and robotics. Now I'm figuring out how to bring those worlds together.

I enjoy the unglamorous parts of engineering—the dataset that doesn't make sense, the sensor that lies to you, the evaluation that reveals an uncomfortable truth. That's usually where the interesting work begins.

I'm especially interested in perception and field-ready AI: the kinds of systems that leave the controlled demo behind and still have to earn someone's trust.
